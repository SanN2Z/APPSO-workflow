# X/Twitter Operations SOP

Last checked: 2026-07-04

## Principle

Operate X as a repeatable business experiment:

Positioning -> original content -> real conversations -> analytics review -> monetization loop.

Do not treat follower growth, likes, or X revenue sharing as the business. The business result is leads, subscribers, customers, sponsors, community members, or product users.

## 1. Positioning

Define these before posting at scale:

- Audience: who should follow this account.
- Problem: what repeated pain or desire the account helps with.
- Offer: what the account can eventually sell or route attention toward.
- Differentiation: why this account is worth reading instead of another account in the same niche.
- Proof: experience, examples, experiments, case studies, or public building logs.

Profile checklist:

- clear avatar;
- concise bio with niche and value;
- one primary link;
- pinned post that explains who the account helps and why;
- no misleading identity or affiliation.

## 2. Baseline Audit

Run this weekly for an active account and once before starting a new sprint.

Metrics:

- followers gained or lost;
- total impressions;
- engagement rate;
- replies from relevant people;
- profile visits;
- link clicks;
- email subscribers or leads;
- sales, calls, trials, or other business conversions.

Content review:

- top 10 posts by impressions;
- top 10 posts by meaningful replies;
- top 10 posts by conversions;
- bottom 10 posts by engagement;
- repeated topics, hooks, and formats.

Output:

- 3 patterns to repeat;
- 3 patterns to stop;
- 1-2 experiments for next week.

## 3. Listening

Maintain watchlists:

- 20-50 benchmark accounts;
- 20 potential customers or target readers;
- 10-20 communities, hashtags, or recurring conversations;
- competing products, newsletters, creators, and agencies.

Daily capture:

- questions people repeatedly ask;
- complaints and unsolved problems;
- posts that trigger high-quality discussion;
- phrases the audience naturally uses;
- offers or CTAs that appear to convert.

The goal is not copying. The goal is to learn demand, vocabulary, and proof standards.

## 4. Content System

Use 3-5 content pillars. Example:

- strong opinions;
- tutorials and workflows;
- case studies or teardown posts;
- curated resources;
- build-in-public updates;
- customer or market observations;
- product or service conversion posts.

Recommended content mix:

- 80% education, insight, entertainment, or community value;
- 20% direct promotion, lead magnet, offer, or sales CTA.

Formats:

- single-post opinion;
- short tactical checklist;
- thread;
- image/chart;
- quote post with added analysis;
- long-form post;
- before/after case study;
- weekly recap.

Avoid making most posts simple outbound links. Prefer native posts that provide value inside X, then use links selectively.

## 5. Daily Operating Loop

Morning:

- scan watchlists for 15-30 minutes;
- capture 5-10 ideas;
- draft 2-5 posts;
- choose 1 priority conversation to join.

Publishing:

- publish reviewed posts only;
- keep CTAs natural and sparse;
- vary post formats;
- log topic, hook, format, CTA, and publishing time.

Engagement:

- reply to high-signal comments first;
- leave useful replies under relevant accounts;
- record potential leads with context;
- avoid generic replies, bait, or copy-paste engagement.

End of day:

- mark which posts had early traction;
- save useful replies or objections;
- convert repeated questions into future post ideas.

## 6. Weekly Experiment Loop

Choose one or two variables per week:

- topic;
- hook style;
- post length;
- posting time;
- media vs text;
- CTA style;
- thread vs single post;
- personal story vs tactical advice.

Do not change everything at once. Each experiment needs:

- hypothesis;
- post set;
- metric;
- result;
- next action.

Example:

- Hypothesis: teardown posts attract more qualified replies than generic AI tips.
- Test: publish 5 teardown posts and 5 generic tips this week.
- Metric: relevant replies, profile visits, follows, link clicks.
- Decision: double down, revise, or stop.

## 7. Monetization Loop

Primary monetization options:

- newsletter;
- consulting;
- calls;
- paid community;
- templates;
- course;
- affiliate;
- sponsorship;
- SaaS or tool leads;
- agency/service pipeline.

Every offer needs:

- target reader;
- promise;
- proof;
- CTA;
- tracking link or manual conversion log.

Do not rely on X creator payouts as the core business model. X's creator revenue sharing requirements and payout rules can change.

## 8. Compliance Boundaries

Do not implement or run:

- fake engagement;
- paid engagement exchange;
- account farming;
- bulk unsolicited replies, mentions, or DMs;
- mass follow/unfollow;
- duplicate posts across many accounts;
- irrelevant hashtag hijacking;
- scraping behind login;
- CAPTCHA bypass;
- rate-limit bypass;
- automated likes;
- automated DM follow-ups without clear user opt-in.

Automation should be human-in-the-loop by default:

- draft suggestions are allowed;
- analytics import is allowed;
- scheduling assistance is allowed;
- posting, replies, DMs, likes, follows, and list actions require explicit approval unless a later compliant production protocol exists.

## 9. Minimum Tooling For This Project

Start with:

- strategy profile;
- idea backlog;
- content calendar;
- post performance table;
- experiment log;
- lead tracker;
- weekly report generator.

Later additions:

- X API integration, only after checking current API pricing, policy, and rate limits;
- browser extension or overlay, only if the workflow needs in-context assistance;
- CRM integration;
- newsletter or landing page integration.
