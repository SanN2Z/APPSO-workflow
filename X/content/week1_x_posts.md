Use these as reviewed drafts. Do not publish any post without explicit
confirmation.

## Positioning

Audience: AI tool builders, indie hackers, consultants, small B2B founders.

Offer: 7-day X Account Launch Sprint.

CTA: DM `XSPRINT`.

## Draft 1: Pain

```text
Your product is not dead.

Your X account just makes it look dead.

Most early products do not need more features.
They need:

- a sharper profile
- a clearer buyer
- 3 repeatable content pillars
- posts that name the pain in the first line
```

## Draft 4: Teardown Angle

```text
I reviewed a few early-stage product accounts on X.

The same problem keeps showing up:

They explain what the product does.
They do not explain why the buyer should care today.

Feature-first posting feels clear to the founder.
Pain-first posting feels relevant to the buyer.
```

Hook score: 25/30

## Draft 5: Before/After Hook

```text
Weak:
"We launched an AI productivity tool."

Stronger:
"Your team does not need another AI tool. It needs fewer status meetings."

The second hook works better because it starts with the buyer's pain, not the founder's shipping event.
```

Hook score: 27/30

## Draft 6: Simple Framework

```text
Every X post for a product should pass 3 tests:

1. Who is this for?
2. What pain does it name?
3. What changes after reading it?

If a post only says "we launched", it fails all 3.
```

Hook score: 23/30

## Draft 7: Anti-Automation Stance

```text
Automating X too early is usually a mistake.

If your positioning is unclear, automation only spreads confusion faster.

Fix the basics first:

- one buyer
- one pain
- one offer
- one weekly experiment loop
```

Hook score: 24/30

## Draft 9: Contrarian

```text
Most founders do not have a posting problem.

They have a translation problem.

They know the product.
They know the features.
They know the roadmap.

But they have not translated any of that into the buyer's language.
```
